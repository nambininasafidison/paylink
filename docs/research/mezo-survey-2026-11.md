| | |

| **Where** | The FaceDev Facebook group of Malagasy developers |

## 6. Honest reading

- **Payments from abroad mostly work for these developers.** 8 of 9 say the last one went fine, 5 of 9 used a plain international bank transfer, and 8 of 9 lost under 3 % or nothing they know of. A product that sells "cheaper, faster cross-border payments" to this group answers a pain most of them do not report.
- **The pains that remain are trust, not transport.** Knowing for sure that money has arrived (1 waited days to be sure; 1 met a client who said "I paid" with nothing sent, a false proof of payment and a dispute on the amount) and converting crypto to ariary through someone trustworthy (1 lost a bitcoin payment to a reseller who never paid). That is what wave 2 builds for: "Was I really paid?" answers from the chain alone, never from the message or the screenshot ([submissions/mezo.md §12](../submissions/mezo.md#12-wave-2-2026-11)). PayLink does nothing about cash-out to ariary, and says so.
- **Willingness to pay is thin.** 2 of 9 pay for a tool; 7 use free tools or none. PayLink stays free to use, with no fee on payments ([submissions/mezo.md §3](../submissions/mezo.md#business-model-and-go-to-market)).
- **Crypto is not foreign to them:** 4 of 9 have received or held crypto or stablecoins. That is a reason to test MUSD with them, not evidence that they want it.

## 7. Bias and limits

- **One channel:** a single Facebook group of developers, so tech-savvy people who already work with foreign clients. Designers, writers and virtual assistants, who may be paid differently, are 2 of 10.
- **Small sample:** 10 responses, 9 for the payment questions. One answer moves a share by 11 points. Nothing here is a percentage of Malagasy freelancers.
- **Self-selected and self-reported:** people chose to answer, and recall their last payment; a bad experience may be over- or under-reported.
- **No follow-up:** answers are not checked against any record, and nobody was interviewed.
